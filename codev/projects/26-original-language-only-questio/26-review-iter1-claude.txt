## Three findings worth folding in (none blocking)

**1. The Arabic-Indic digit open question is actually resolved — but recorded as unresolved.** The spec listed this as an "Important" open question with a required test scenario; the review demotes it to a follow-up. I measured it: **0 of 1,427 answers contain any Arabic-Indic digit**, and **0 incorrect non-English `cite_passage` cells contain the correct citation verbatim**. So the by-example exemplar steer worked, and the low Arabic numbers (opus 18%, haiku 14%) are genuine model failure, not a scorer artifact. That's a real validity result for a Phase-1 paper claim — put it in the run README and close the follow-up, and pin the digit case in a cheap unit test (the spec's Test Scenarios asked for exactly that assertion).

**2. `parse_yes_no` conflates format-noncompliance with refusal.** Clear, committed negative verdicts phrased in prose are bucketed `refused` and excluded from accuracy entirely: `"Not from this edition."`, `"The answer is no."`, `"That is incorrect."`, `"This text is not from the KJV; it is from the Book of Mormon."` all parse to `None`. Because `verify_source` is the false-premise instrument and the negatives carry the signal, a future model that rejects in prose would have its *correct* rejections dropped from the denominator — inflating its reported accuracy. Zero impact on this run (0/357 refused; every answer led with Yes/No), and it is the contract both consult reviewers converged on, so I'm not asking you to change it. But the review's follow-up should say "prose verdicts are excluded, a measurement risk for future panels," not merely "the refusal path is unexercised."

**3. A docstring overstates the negative-exclusion rule.** `generate.py` (line ~45) and the spec both say negatives exclude passages present in the asked edition by "**containment** over all entries," naming BoM-quoting-KJV-Isaiah as the case it catches. The implementation is `ne in asked.all_norm` — membership in a frozenset of *whole* normalized entry texts, i.e. equality, not substring containment. A passage quoted verbatim inside a longer unit would not be excluded. I tested the committed run under the stronger substring rule: **0 negatives leak**, so no evidence is affected. Fix the wording (cheaper and probably correct for entry-granular sampling) rather than the code.

One non-issue for the record: the shared RNG means a text's sampled items depend on preceding texts and on the active task set, so `--tasks` subsets yield different per-text questions. Deterministic and digest-pinned — worth knowing, not worth changing. And the remaining `identify_version`/`identify_source` strings are only in explanatory comments and absence-asserting tests, which is correct.
